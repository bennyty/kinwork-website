The client is an international bead business [or in the jewelry business] that purchases beads from suppliers around the world and sells them to customers through an online store.
The business maintains a large inventory of products. Each product requires several pieces of information to be organized and entered into the store, including:
- SKU Number
- Product photographs
- Product descriptions
- Material
- Location or source
- Pricing

As the inventory grew, managing this information became increasingly manual and time-consuming.

# The Challenge:
The client's existing workflow required significant manual file handling and data entry. For each new product, information and photographs had to be located, organized, labeled, and manually entered into the store. This meant the owner was spending substantial time:
- Searching through folders for product photographs.
- Identifying and selecting the photograph(s) that meet their standards.
- Manually entering and sometimes re-entering product information.
- Connecting product information to the correct item in the online store.
- Repeating these steps for each individual product.

# The Solution:
We developed a custom application designed specifically around the client's inventory workflow. Rather than treating each task as a separate process, we created a more continuous workflow that allowed the owner to move efficiently from one product to the next.
1. Grouping Products by Bead

The application organizes products by bead, making it easier to manage related inventory and work through products systematically.

The client's product IDs already contained useful information, including details such as the material and location associated with the bead. However, this information was not being fully leveraged to reduce manual work. The result was a workflow that involved a lot of repetitive file searching, typing, and product-by-product organization.

This application reduced the need to repeatedly look up or manually re-enter information that was already available.
2. Faster Photo Selection
The owner needed to choose the photograph that would ultimately represent each product in the store.
We created a custom photo-selection tool that allowed the owner to quickly review photographs and either reject unwanted images or select the preferred photograph.
Instead of manually opening and searching through folders, the owner could make photo decisions directly within a single program and the next product photo would automatically be pulled up for continuous workflow.
3. Automatic Metadata Entry
Product metadata was captured and automatically connected to the online store as a draft.
Relevant information could be attached and mapped to the appropriate store fields, reducing the amount of manual data entry required.
This helped turn information that had already been captured into usable product data without requiring the owner to repeatedly type meta data for each individual bread.
Summary of Three Major Time-Saving Improvements
Photos: A dedicated interface made it faster to review, reject, and select product photographs.
Mapping: Existing product information, including information contained within the Product ID, could be mapped to the appropriate store fields instead of being manually entered repeatedly.
Dedicated Workspace: The custom application created a continuous workspace for processing inventory. (Instead of searching through folders, switching between files, and figuring out what product to work on next, the owner could follow a streamlined workflow from one product to the next.)

# The Result
The solution transformed a fragmented, manual inventory process into a more structured and streamlined workflow.
The main improvement was not simply automating one individual task. Instead, the application connected several steps—photo selection, product information, metadata, and store preparation—into one continuous process.
This reduced repetitive work and allowed the owner to spend less time managing files and entering information manually, while maintaining greater consistency in how products were prepared for the online store.


Kinwork’s found a way to turn a repetitive, file-heavy inventory process into a dedicated workflow that helps the client move from product photography to store-ready inventory with fewer manual steps.
The project demonstrates how a custom application can be designed around the way a business actually works, rather than forcing the business to adapt to a generic workflow.
